\section{Persuading the Scribe}
\label{sec:scribePersuasion}

If the party believes they should be granted the entry without the paperwork due to their status and/or priority of their mission, read the following:

\quoteBox{
	"I understand the importance of your assignment", the guard says, "but the legal procedures of the city require all people with an immigrant status to present proper documentation. Perhaps my colleague can explain the procedure in more detail. \npcNissiqqan{}, could you please come over here?"
}

The party enters a Conversation with \npcHauka{}, a \enemyGuardC{} and \npcNissiqqan{}, a \enemyScribeB{} of Immigration. Both of them must be convinced to let the party in, before the PCs can proceed.


\subsubsection{Hauka, a \enemyGuardNameC{}}

\conversationDefence{\enemyGuardC{}}{16}{17}{5}
She is pragmatic and reliable guard captain, with a lot of respect for law and bureaucratic procedures. 

Being from Azir, she defers to the local authorities and will need to spend 2 Focus to resist influence after \enemyScribeB{} backed down from the conversation.

After recent events, she will see Surgebending and other supernatural powers as trouble that should be avoided. Any attempts to influence her that rely on impressing her with such powers will always fail.

\conversationEnd{\enemyGuardC{}}{1}


\subsubsection{Nissiqqan, a \enemyScribeNameB{} of Immigration}

\conversationDefence{\enemyScribeB{}}{16}{16}{5}
He wishes to restore order to his city in those times of chaos and uncertainty, and he believes that forms and taxes are the best method to do it.

\conversationEnd{\enemyScribeB{}}{1}



\subsection{Resolving the Conversation}

After both \npcHauka{} and \npcNissiqqan{} back down from the Conversation, the PCs are allowed to enter the city. Proceed to \fullref{sec:enteredCity}.

If the PCs failed to convince them, they will need to find another way to enter \locationYeddaw{}...
