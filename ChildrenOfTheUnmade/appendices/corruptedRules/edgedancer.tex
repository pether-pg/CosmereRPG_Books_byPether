\section{Enlightened Edgedancer}

\corruptedBelief{I will remember.}{\corruptedBeliefEdgedancer{}}

\subsection{Philosophy Changes}

Enlightened Edgedancers realize that mere compassion and memory of the forgotten does not fix their situation in the long run. Instead, they focus on the source of their misfortune, never yielding until all wrongs are made right and ensured to never happen again.

At their best, those Edgedancers are focus-driven Radiants, determined to improve the lives of common people.

At their worst, they can become petty, unforgiving individuals with little compassion and understanding in their hearts.

\subsection{Enlightened Cultivationspren}

In the Physical Realm, Enlightened cultivationspren reassemble a skull overgrown with entangled red vines tailing behind it and dripping blood-like sap.

In the Cognitive Realm, those vines form a full human-like body. The spren wear rags in here, soaked with red sap still dripping from the vines.

\subsection{Drawbacks and Complications}

Progression and Enlightened Edgedancer's talents cause a terrible pain. The GM can request a \skillDiscipline{} check (\corruptedResistanceDC{} by the Edgedancer) from the person being affected by those talents. On a failure, they lose 1 Focus.

Enlightened Edgedancers can be tempted to hurt, maim or even kill other creatures only to experiment with their abilities. When the GM tempts an Edgedancer like that, they must pass a Discipline check (\corruptedResistanceDC{}) or spend a Focus point. If they don't, they must take a hostile action against that creature.

\talent{\talentEdgedancerSpeakWithDeadName{}}
		{Speak the First Ideal; \talentEdgedancerKeyName{} talent}
		{\iconAction}
		{Through the vines the dead can speak with you.}
		{Spend 1 Investiture to sprout red vines around a dead creature. You Connect to it, allowing you to speak with it as though it was an NPC. You may talk with it even if the creature did not know your language or was incapable of speaking at all.
			
		The effect lasts for a number of rounds equal to your number of ranks in Progression. During each of those rounds you may ask the corpse one question. The corpse will answer to the best of its knowledge unless it recognizes you as their killer.}
		
\talent{\talentEdgedancerEnhanceName}
		{Speak the First Ideal; \talentEdgedancerKeyName{} talent}
		{\iconActionDouble}
		{The vines pierce their skin and enhance their body.}
		{Choose a creature or a corpse. You can spend Investiture to accelerate the growth of vines, which will pierce their skin and grow over their body, granting them bonus Strength. Both Investiture cost and the amount of bonus Strength granted are proportional to the size of the creature (1~for Small creatures, 2 for Medium and so on).
		
		The bonus affects all Strength derivatives (Physical Defense, Health and skills) and lasts for a number of days equal to your ranks in Progression. 
		
		If that creature was previously under the effect of \talentEdgedancerKeyName{} talent, this action costs 1 less Investiture.}
		
\talent{\talentEdgedancerPuppeteerName}
		{\talentEdgedancerEnhanceName{} talent}
		{\iconActionDouble}
		{Your red vines can overgrow and subdue other creatures}
		{Touch a creature and spend Investiture to make a Progression check against its Spiritual Defense. If you succeed, you gain control over the creature as your red vines entangle its body and break its spirit.
			
		The amount of Investiture required to make a check is proportional to the size of the creature (1 for Small creatures, 2 for Medium and so on).
			
		After a number of rounds equal to your ranks in Progression, repeat the Progression check. The creature breaks free if the check fails. }
